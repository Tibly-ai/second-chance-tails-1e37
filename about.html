Our mission

The last day on a list becomes the first day of a life.

Second Chance Tails Rescue started with one van, two crates, and a phone call from a county shelter that had run out of room. We have not stopped driving since.

The story behind the barn

We built this around the animals nobody had room for.

Rural shelters in our region run on small budgets and smaller staff. When kennels fill, a euthanasia list gets written, usually on a Friday. Most of the names on it belong to healthy, ordinary animals: a senior beagle whose owner passed away, a litter of barn kittens, a shepherd mix who barks at the wrong time.

Our founder, Marguerite, spent nine years as a shelter tech before she started answering those Friday calls herself. The rescue now runs out of a converted dairy barn outside town with a quarantine wing, a cat sunroom, and a paddock where dogs learn that grass is safe.

Every animal that comes through here is vetted, vaccinated, microchipped, and known by name before a single adoption photo is taken. We would rather hold a dog two months longer than send them to a house that is wrong for them.

How a pull actually works

01

The call comes in

A partner shelter sends us their list with photos, intake notes, and a deadline. We answer within the hour, seven days a week.

02

Transport and triage

Volunteer drivers run the route. Our vet partner sees every animal within 48 hours for exam, vaccines, spay or neuter, and dental if needed.

03

Decompression in foster

Two quiet weeks in a home, on a couch or in a sunny window. Fosters tell us who this animal really is, which is what makes the match work.

04

The right family, not the first

Applications, a phone conversation, a meet and greet at the barn. We stay reachable for the life of the animal, no exceptions.
