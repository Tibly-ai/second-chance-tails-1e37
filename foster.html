Foster & Volunteer

A spare room is the shortest road home.

When a rural shelter posts a euthanasia list, the animals on it do not need a miracle. They need a bed that is not concrete and someone who can answer questions about them. That is what a foster home is. You keep the dog or cat for a few weeks, you learn who they are, and we use what you learn to find the family that fits.

Volunteers do the rest of the work that keeps the pipeline moving: transport runs, laundry, weekend meet and greets, photos that actually look like the animal. No experience required for most of it. We will teach you the parts that need teaching.

You bring the couch. We cover the rest.

Fostering should not cost you money or leave you guessing. Everything below is handled by the rescue, for every animal in every foster home.

Medical

Vet bills, all of them

Vaccines, spay or neuter, heartworm testing, dental work, the surprise ear infection at 9pm. Our partner clinics bill us directly. You never hand over a card.

Supplies

Crate, food, litter, leash

Pick it up from the barn pantry or we drop it at your door on the transport route. If your foster eats a prescription diet, we buy that too.

Support

A person, not a form

Every foster gets a coordinator by name and a text line that stays open evenings and weekends. Housetraining setbacks and barrier barking are normal. Ask early.

Training

Behavior help included

Our trainer runs a free Saturday session at the lounge and does home visits for dogs coming off a hard shelter stay. Reward based, no shortcuts.

Adoption work

We screen the applicants

Reference checks, vet history, home conversations, contracts. Your job is to tell us the truth about your foster. Ours is to find the family that matches it.

Time off

Vacation coverage

Wedding, work trip, rough week. Give us a few days notice and a backup foster or a boarding partner takes over. Fostering does not mean you stop having a life.

Cannot foster? There is still a shift with your name on it.

Most volunteers give two to four hours a week. Some give one Saturday a month. Both matter.

  • Transport driver Two to three hour legs between partner shelters and foster homes, usually Friday mornings. We fuel the vehicle.
  • Lounge shifts Cleaning, feeding, cat socializing, greeting families who walk in on a Sunday afternoon with no appointment.
  • Photos and video A good photo moves an animal off the list faster than anything else we do. Phone cameras are fine.
  • Event crew Setup and teardown at farmers market adoption days, plus holding leashes and answering the same twelve questions kindly.
  • Home checks A friendly half hour visit with an approved adopter, checklist provided. Great fit for retired volunteers.
  • Laundry and supply runs Blankets, towels, crate pads. Pick up a bin, wash it at home, bring it back. Genuinely one of our most needed roles.

How fostering runs

From the shelter list to your door in about a week.

  1. 01

    Apply and talk it through

    The form takes about ten minutes. Then a coordinator calls you. We ask about your other pets, your work hours, your stairs, your fence. There is no wrong answer, only a better match.

  2. 02

    Quick home walkthrough

    In person or over video, whichever is easier. We are looking at where the crate goes and how a nervous dog gets outside, not at your housekeeping.

  3. 03

    We match you to an animal

    You tell us your limits up front: no cats, no large dogs, no bottle babies, weekdays only. We hold to them. A foster placement that fails helps nobody, least of all the animal.

  4. 04

    Pickup day

    Your foster arrives vetted, microchipped, and already named. Supplies come with them. Plan on a quiet first two days with the crate open and the house calm.

  5. 05

    Two to eight weeks of ordinary life

    Walks, meals, a vet recheck if needed, and a short weekly note from you about how they handle kids, stairs, thunder, the vacuum. That note is what sells them to the right family.

  6. 06

    The handoff

    You meet the adopters. You get a say. Most fosters cry, then take a week off, then text us asking who is next. Adopters usually keep sending you photos for years.
