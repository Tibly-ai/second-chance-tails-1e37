Some of them have never had a name for more than a week.

Every dog and cat on our adoption list came off a euthanasia hold at a rural shelter and has been living in a foster home since. That means we can tell you how they handle a doorbell, a vacuum, a toddler, and a thunderstorm before you ever fill out a form.

Adoption takes about two weeks from application to the ride home. Nothing about it is a race. We would rather place an animal once than twice.

The first ten steps into grass are the ones we work for.

How adoption works

Four steps, one coordinator, and honest answers about the animal in front of you. If a match is wrong for either of you, we say so early.

Questions before you apply? Call (555) 014-2288 or write to hello@secondchancetails.org.

  1. 01

    Send the application

    Ten minutes of typing. Who lives in the house, whether it is a fenced yard or a third-floor walk-up, what happened to your last pet, and which animal caught your eye. Couch sleeping is allowed here, so answer honestly.

  2. 02

    A call with your coordinator

    About twenty minutes on the phone within three days. We check your vet reference, talk through the animal's real quirks, and sort out landlord paperwork if you rent. This is also where you ask us the awkward questions.

  3. 03

    Meet and greet

    An hour at our adoption lounge or at the foster family's home, whichever suits the animal. Bring everyone who lives with you, including the resident dog on a leash. Most visits end with somebody sitting on the floor.

  4. 04

    Homecoming day

    Sign the adoption contract, take the medical file, and drive home with two weeks of the food they already eat. Your foster's phone number comes with them, and our team checks in at day three, day thirty, and any time you need us after that.

What the fee covers

Spay or neuter, age-appropriate vaccines, deworming, microchip registered in your name, and a heartworm or FeLV test. Fees vary by animal and are listed on each profile.

Bringing kids or a resident pet

Cats meet new households in a quiet room. Dog-to-dog introductions happen outside on neutral ground with two handlers, never over a fence line.

If it is not the right fit

Say so at the visit. No guilt, no hard sell. We will show you the two or three animals in foster care who actually match your week.
